Biology: the cell

Revision for the biology exam. Cards are in the table below; the app keeps when each is due.

## Notes

The cell theory: all living things are made of cells, the cell is the basic unit of life, and new cells come from existing cells.

Eukaryotic cells have a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Prokaryotic cells, like bacteria, have neither; their DNA is a circular chromosome in the nucleoid.

Vacuole: a sac that stores water, nutrients and waste. In plant cells one large central vacuole keeps the cell firm.
Centrioles: pairs of microtubule cylinders that organise the spindle during cell division in animal cells.
Peroxisomes: small organelles that break down fatty acids and hydrogen peroxide.
Tight junctions are protein seals between cells that stop fluid leaking between them.
Facilitated diffusion is diffusion through channel or carrier proteins, without energy.

Cell division: interphase, then mitosis, then cytokinesis, when the cytoplasm splits in two.

## Cards

| Front | Back |
| --- | --- |
| What does the mitochondrion do? | Makes most of the cell's ATP through cellular respiration. |
| Where does glycolysis happen? | In the cytoplasm. |
| What is the job of ribosomes? | They build proteins from amino acids, reading mRNA. |
| Rough vs smooth ER? | Rough ER has ribosomes and folds proteins; smooth ER makes lipids and breaks down toxins. |
| What does the Golgi apparatus do? | Modifies, sorts and packages proteins into vesicles. |
| What do lysosomes contain? | Digestive enzymes that break down waste and worn-out parts. |
| Name three things plant cells have that animal cells don't. | A cell wall, chloroplasts and a large central vacuole. |
| What is the cell membrane made of? | A phospholipid bilayer with proteins and cholesterol in it. |
| What is diffusion? | Movement of particles from high to low concentration, without energy. |
| What is osmosis? | Diffusion of water through a semi-permeable membrane. |
| Active transport needs what? | Energy (ATP) and carrier proteins, to move against the gradient. |
| What happens in interphase? | The cell grows and copies its DNA (G1, S, G2). |
| The four stages of mitosis? | Prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase. |
| What happens in anaphase? | Sister chromatids are pulled to opposite poles. |
| Mitosis vs meiosis? | Mitosis gives two identical diploid cells; meiosis gives four different haploid cells. |
| What is a prokaryote? | A cell without a nucleus or membrane-bound organelles, like bacteria. |
| Where is DNA in a prokaryote? | In a single circular chromosome in the nucleoid, plus plasmids. |
| What does the nucleolus make? | Ribosomal RNA and ribosome subunits. |
| What is the cytoskeleton? | A network of protein fibres that gives the cell shape and moves things inside it. |
| What does a chloroplast do? | Photosynthesis: makes glucose from light, water and carbon dioxide. |
| What is the formula for photosynthesis? | 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light → C6H12O6 + 6 O2 |
| How much ATP does aerobic respiration make per glucose? | About 30 to 32 ATP. |
| What are enzymes? | Proteins that speed up reactions by lowering the activation energy. |
| What denatures an enzyme? | High temperature or the wrong pH changes its shape, so the substrate no longer fits. |
